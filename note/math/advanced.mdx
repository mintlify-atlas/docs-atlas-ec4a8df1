University mathematics is a substantial step beyond the high school curriculum in both abstraction and rigor. Where high school math teaches you to compute, university math increasingly asks you to prove — to understand *why* results are true, not just *how* to apply them. These notes cover the core subjects encountered in a typical Chinese university STEM program, with an emphasis on building genuine understanding rather than exam-only pattern matching.

## Calculus

Calculus is the study of continuous change. It is split into two main branches — differential calculus (rates of change) and integral calculus (accumulation) — unified by the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus.

### Limits & Continuity

A limit describes the value a function approaches as its input approaches some value. Formally, $\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = L$ means that $f(x)$ can be made arbitrarily close to $L$ by taking $x$ sufficiently close to $a$.

Key results:
- Squeeze theorem
- L'Hôpital's rule for indeterminate forms ($0/0$, $\infty/\infty$)
- Continuity and the Intermediate Value Theorem

### Differential Calculus

The **derivative** $f'(x)$ measures the instantaneous rate of change of $f$ at $x$. Geometrically, it is the slope of the tangent line to the curve.

Core differentiation rules:

| Rule | Formula |
|------|---------|
| Power rule | $(x^n)' = nx^{n-1}$ |
| Product rule | $(fg)' = f'g + fg'$ |
| Quotient rule | $(f/g)' = (f'g - fg')/g^2$ |
| Chain rule | $(f \circ g)' = f'(g) \cdot g'$ |

Common derivatives: $(\sin x)' = \cos x$, $(\cos x)' = -\sin x$, $(e^x)' = e^x$, $(\ln x)' = 1/x$.

Applications: finding maxima and minima, the Mean Value Theorem, Taylor series approximation.

### Integral Calculus

The **definite integral** $\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$ measures the net signed area under a curve. The **Fundamental Theorem of Calculus** connects differentiation and integration:

$$
\frac{d}{dx}\int_a^x f(t)\,dt = f(x)
$$

Integration techniques: substitution, integration by parts ($\int u\,dv = uv - \int v\,du$), partial fractions, trigonometric substitution.

Improper integrals extend the definition to unbounded intervals or unbounded integrands.

### Series

An infinite series $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n$ converges if its partial sums approach a finite limit. Key tests: comparison, ratio, root, integral. Power series and Taylor/Maclaurin series represent functions as infinite polynomials:

$$
e^x = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{n!}, \quad \sin x = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n x^{2n+1}}{(2n+1)!}
$$

### Multivariable Calculus

Extends single-variable ideas to functions of several variables: partial derivatives, gradient vectors, double and triple integrals, and the theorems of Green, Stokes, and Gauss.

## Linear Algebra

Linear algebra studies linear equations, vector spaces, and linear maps. It is arguably the most practically useful branch of mathematics — appearing in machine learning, physics, computer graphics, and virtually every area of applied mathematics.

### Matrices & Systems of Equations

A **matrix** is a rectangular array of numbers. Systems of linear equations $Ax = b$ are solved using Gaussian elimination (row reduction to echelon form).

Key matrix operations: addition, scalar multiplication, matrix multiplication (non-commutative in general), transposition.

### Determinants

The **determinant** $\det(A)$ of a square matrix is a scalar that encodes geometric and algebraic information. $\det(A) \neq 0$ if and only if $A$ is invertible.

Properties: $\det(AB) = \det(A)\det(B)$, $\det(A^T) = \det(A)$, row operations affect the determinant predictably.

Computing: $2\times 2$ case directly, larger cases via cofactor expansion or row reduction.

### Vector Spaces

A **vector space** is a set with addition and scalar multiplication satisfying eight axioms. Subspaces, span, linear independence, bases, and dimension are the core structural concepts.

The **rank** of a matrix equals the dimension of its column space (and row space). Rank-nullity theorem: $\text{rank}(A) + \text{nullity}(A) = n$.

### Eigenvalues & Eigenvectors

A nonzero vector $v$ is an **eigenvector** of $A$ with **eigenvalue** $\lambda$ if $Av = \lambda v$. Eigenvalues are roots of the characteristic polynomial $\det(A - \lambda I) = 0$.

Diagonalization: if $A$ has $n$ linearly independent eigenvectors, then $A = PDP^{-1}$ where $D$ is diagonal. This simplifies computing $A^k$ and understanding the long-run behavior of linear systems.

The **spectral theorem** states that real symmetric matrices are always diagonalizable with an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors.

## Probability Theory & Mathematical Statistics

### Probability Spaces

A probability space $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)$ consists of a sample space $\Omega$, a $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal{F}$ of events, and a probability measure $P$.

Classical probability assigns $P(A) = |A|/|\Omega|$ for equally likely outcomes. Geometric probability assigns probability proportional to measure (length, area, volume).

### Random Variables & Distributions

A **random variable** $X$ maps outcomes to real numbers. Key distributions:

| Distribution | Use case |
|-------------|----------|
| Bernoulli$(p)$ | Single trial with success probability $p$ |
| Binomial$(n, p)$ | Count of successes in $n$ independent trials |
| Geometric$(p)$ | Number of trials until first success |
| Poisson$(\lambda)$ | Count of rare events in a fixed interval |
| Normal$(\mu, \sigma^2)$ | Continuous; the bell curve; central limit theorem |
| Exponential$(\lambda)$ | Waiting time between Poisson events |

### Expectation & Variance

$E[X] = \sum x \cdot P(X=x)$ (discrete) or $\int x f(x)\,dx$ (continuous).

$\text{Var}(X) = E[(X - \mu)^2] = E[X^2] - (E[X])^2$.

The **Central Limit Theorem**: for large $n$, the sample mean $\bar{X}$ is approximately normal regardless of the underlying distribution.

## Elementary Number Theory

Number theory studies the integers and their properties. It is the oldest branch of pure mathematics, yet many of its questions remain unsolved.

### Divisibility & Primes

$a \mid b$ means $a$ divides $b$ (i.e., $b = ka$ for some integer $k$). The **greatest common divisor** $\gcd(a,b)$ is computed efficiently by the **Euclidean algorithm**.

The **Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic**: every integer $n > 1$ factors uniquely into primes (up to order).

### Congruences

$a \equiv b \pmod{m}$ means $m \mid (a - b)$. Congruences support arithmetic operations and enable modular arithmetic.

Key results: **Fermat's Little Theorem** ($a^{p-1} \equiv 1 \pmod{p}$ for prime $p$ and $\gcd(a,p)=1$), **Euler's Theorem** ($a^{\phi(n)} \equiv 1 \pmod{n}$), **Chinese Remainder Theorem** (simultaneous congruences with coprime moduli have a unique solution mod their product).

### Connections to Contest Mathematics

Many competition problems rely on number theory: divisibility arguments, modular arithmetic, Diophantine equations, and properties of prime factorizations. See the contest mathematics section for problem-oriented coverage.

## Discrete Mathematics

Discrete mathematics studies structures that are fundamentally countable or finite — the mathematical backbone of computer science.

### Logic & Proof

Propositional logic, predicate logic, proof techniques (direct, contradiction, contrapositive, induction), and the language of mathematical writing.

### Graph Theory

A **graph** $G = (V, E)$ consists of vertices and edges. Key concepts: degree, paths, cycles, connectivity, trees, Euler circuits, Hamiltonian paths, planarity, and graph coloring.

### Combinatorics

Counting via the addition and multiplication principles, permutations, combinations, the inclusion-exclusion principle, generating functions, and recurrence relations.
