## Setup Philosophy

A good study and coding desk setup has three properties:

1. **Ergonomic** — the setup doesn't hurt you after extended use
2. **Distraction-minimized** — the visual environment supports focus, not fragmentation
3. **Sustainable** — you can maintain and evolve the setup without constant effort

These three goals sometimes conflict. A clean, wireless desk looks great but requires charging discipline. A heavily customized setup looks impressive but can become a maintenance burden. Start with ergonomics as the non-negotiable baseline, then work outward.

## Monitor

The monitor is the single most important peripheral for a desk-based knowledge worker. You look at it for hours every day.

### Key Specifications

| Spec | Recommendation | Notes |
|------|---------------|-------|
| Size | 27–32" | Larger than laptop screen; not so large that it requires head movement |
| Resolution | 4K (3840×2160) | Text clarity matters for long reading and coding sessions |
| Panel type | IPS or OLED | IPS: consistent colors, good viewing angles; OLED: perfect blacks, better contrast |
| Refresh rate | 60–120Hz | 60Hz is fine for productivity; higher refresh rates are smoother |
| Brightness | 300–500 nits (SDR) | HDR peaks higher; important for mixed-light environments |
| Color accuracy | sRGB ≥99% | Important for design work; less critical for pure coding/text |

### Ergonomic Positioning

- **Eye level:** The top edge of the monitor should be at or slightly below eye level. Looking down slightly is more comfortable than looking up.
- **Distance:** Roughly arm's length from your eyes — about 50–70cm for a 27" screen.
- **Tilt:** Slight backward tilt (10–15°) reduces glare and maintains a natural neck position.
- **Monitor arm vs. stand:** A monitor arm frees up desk space and allows precise adjustment; the built-in stand is adequate if it reaches the right height for you.

### Single Monitor vs. Dual Monitor

For study (reading, writing, problem-solving): a single large monitor is generally better — fewer distractions, more focus, less neck movement.

For development and multitasking: dual monitors can help by keeping reference material on one screen while working on the other. The key is that the screens are at the same height and your primary monitor is directly in front of you.

## Keyboard

The keyboard is your primary input device. It is worth investing in a quality one.

### Mechanical vs. Membrane

**Mechanical keyboards** use individual switches for each key. They are more precise, more durable (rated for 50–100 million keystrokes), and more customizable. The typing feel and sound vary significantly between switch types.

| Switch type | Feel | Sound | Good for |
|------------|------|-------|---------|
| Linear (e.g., Red) | Smooth, no bump | Quiet | Fast typing, gaming |
| Tactile (e.g., Brown) | Tactile bump at actuation | Moderate | Typing, coding |
| Clicky (e.g., Blue) | Bump + audible click | Loud | Typing, if noise is acceptable |

**Membrane keyboards** use a rubber membrane. They are quieter and cheaper, but less precise and less durable. Fine for light use.

### Layout Considerations

- **Full-size (100%):** Includes numpad. Useful for data entry; takes up more desk space.
- **Tenkeyless (80%):** Removes numpad. Allows mouse closer to the center — better for most use cases.
- **65% or 60%:** Ultra-compact. Requires function-layer key combinations; great for minimal setups.

### Wireless vs. Wired

Wireless (Bluetooth or 2.4GHz dongle) eliminates cable clutter. For study and coding use, modern wireless keyboards have imperceptible latency. The trade-off is battery management — either rechargeable (USB-C, convenient) or AA batteries (long-lasting but requires spares).

## Mouse / Pointing Device

### Mouse

For extended desk use, an ergonomic mouse is worth the investment. Key factors:

- **Weight:** Lighter mice reduce wrist fatigue during long sessions. Sub-80g is considered light.
- **Shape:** Right-hand ergonomic shapes support a natural hand position; ambidextrous shapes work for both hands.
- **DPI:** 800–1600 DPI is suitable for most monitor sizes. Higher DPI allows faster cursor movement with less physical movement.

### Trackpad

An Apple Magic Trackpad provides gesture control well-integrated with macOS. Suitable as a primary pointing device for workflows that involve a lot of swiping and gesture-based navigation. Less precise for pixel-level design work.

## Ergonomics

### Chair

The chair is the foundation of an ergonomic setup. An inadequate chair creates persistent back and neck pain that is difficult to resolve even with a perfect desk arrangement.

Key features of a good ergonomic chair:
- **Lumbar support:** Adjustable support for the natural inward curve of the lower back
- **Armrests:** Height-adjustable; support forearms at desk level to reduce shoulder tension
- **Seat depth and height:** Both adjustable to match your leg proportions
- **Backrest angle:** Should recline slightly under load — a 100–110° angle is common for sustained comfort

### Desk Height

The standard desk height of 720–750mm suits people roughly 170–180cm tall in a seated position. For those outside this range, or for standing desk use, an adjustable-height desk is the correct solution.

**Correct posture at desk:**
- Feet flat on the floor (or on a footrest)
- Knees at roughly 90°
- Elbows at desk height — forearms parallel to the floor or slightly downward
- Screen at or slightly below eye level

### Standing Desk

Alternating between sitting and standing throughout the day reduces the physical load of sustained sitting. A motorized standing desk allows quick transitions. The general recommendation is not to stand all day (which creates its own problems) but to switch positions every 60–90 minutes.

## Lighting

### Ambient Lighting

Poor lighting causes eye strain and fatigue. The goal is to minimize the contrast between the bright screen and the surrounding environment.

- **Bias lighting:** LED strips behind the monitor that emit a soft glow matching the screen's color temperature. Reduces eye strain by reducing the contrast between the screen and the dark wall behind it.
- **Color temperature:** Warm white (2700–3000K) in the evening; cooler daylight (4000–5000K) during the day for alertness.
- **Avoid direct glare:** Position your desk so windows are to the side, not directly in front of or behind the monitor.

### Task Lighting

A desk lamp with adjustable color temperature and brightness for focused task lighting. A high CRI (Color Rendering Index) value (≥90) ensures colors look natural under the lamp — important for design work.

**Monitor light bar** (屏幕挂灯): A lamp that clips to the top of the monitor. Illuminates the desk area below without shining into your eyes or reflecting off the screen. Popular in the Chinese desk setup community for their elegance and effectiveness.

## Cable Management

Cable clutter is the biggest visual enemy of a clean desk.

**Strategies:**
1. **Go wireless where possible** — keyboard, mouse, speakers. Fewer cables = fewer problems.
2. **Monitor riser / desk shelf** — elevates the monitor to eye height and provides a space underneath to hide cables and peripherals (docking stations, hubs).
3. **Cable raceways** — channels that route cables along desk edges or legs, hiding them from view.
4. **Velcro cable ties** — reusable, adjustable ties that bundle loose cables cleanly.
5. **Under-desk cable tray** — mounts to the underside of the desk to hold power strips and excess cable length out of sight.

## Ambient Atmosphere

Beyond functional requirements, the desk environment affects mood and focus:

- **Plants:** A small plant (succulent, snake plant, or pothos) adds life to an otherwise digital workspace. Low-maintenance species that tolerate indoor conditions are practical choices.
- **Color palette:** A coherent color scheme for desk accessories (mat, lamp, headphone stand, plant pot) creates visual calm. Contrast and clutter compete for visual attention; harmony supports focus.
- **Sound:** Some people focus better with ambient noise (brown noise, rain sounds, lo-fi music). Others need silence. Knowing which you are and designing for it matters.

## 3D Planning

Before purchasing furniture or committing to a layout, modeling the desk setup in 3D is an efficient way to visualize the space and avoid costly mistakes. Tools suitable for this:

- **Blender** — free, full-featured 3D modeling; steep learning curve
- **SketchUp** — more accessible for architectural/furniture planning
- **Rhinoceros** — professional NURBS modeling; the standard in product design

For a desk setup specifically, SketchUp's parametric room planning approach is often the most efficient. Many furniture manufacturers provide 3D models of their products that can be imported directly.
